## ✍️ Exam technique

- **1 mark ≈ 1 minute ≈ 1 solid point.** A 4-mark question wants 4 distinct points, not one long paragraph.
- **Always define the term first** before discussing it — definitions carry marks on their own.
- **Use bullet points and bold headings.** Examiners tick points; make them easy to find.
- **Quote a number or a name whenever you can** — "ILO C155 of 1981", "85 dBA over an 8-hour TWA", "€2.2 per €1 invested", "Factories Ordinance No. 45 of 1942". These separate a pass from a distinction.
- **Add a Sri Lankan example** wherever the question allows — the paper is written for a Sri Lankan context.
- **For "explain" questions:** *what it is → why it matters → an example.*
- **For case studies:** quote directly from the case as your evidence ("*confused patients sometimes resisted, increasing the strain*") — it proves you read it.
- **Never leave a question blank.** Even a definition and one bullet earns marks.
- **Attempt every part.** Four half-answered 25-mark questions beat two perfect ones.
