## 1. The 4 core definitions (MEMORISE WORD-FOR-WORD)

| Term | Definition | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|
| **Health** (WHO) | "A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." | **P-M-S** = Physical, Mental, Social. "Not merely absence of disease" |
| **Safety** | Preventing harm & ensuring well-being. OSHA: conditions "reasonably necessary or appropriate to provide safe or healthful employment". ILO: discipline preventing work-related injuries/diseases | Safety = **active process**, not "no accidents" |
| **Hazard** | Any **source** with potential to cause damage, harm or adverse health effects | Hazard = the **gun** |
| **Risk** | ISO 31000: "the **effect of uncertainty on objectives**". OHS: combination of **likelihood** of a hazardous event × **severity** of harm | Risk = the **chance of being shot** |

> **Formula to write in every exam answer:** `Risk = Likelihood × Severity`

**Exam-ready example pair (Q2a asks exactly this):**
- Hazard: a wet floor in a hotel lobby / an unguarded circular saw / a bottle of hydrochloric acid.
- Risk: the *probability a guest slips and fractures a hip* / *probability of finger amputation* / *probability of chemical burns from a splash*.

### Why define terms carefully? (5 reasons — mnemonic **C-L-E-M-S**)
1. **C**larity and consistency (shared language)
2. **L**egal & regulatory compliance (OSHA/ILO definitions underpin the law)
3. **E**ffective risk assessment (can't assess what you can't name)
4. **M**eaningful data collection & analysis (comparable incident data)
5. **S**trong safety culture (common language → shared commitment)

## 2. The 6 hazard categories (guaranteed exam question)

Mnemonic: **"Please Come By Every Safe Person"** → **P**hysical, **C**hemical, **B**iological, **E**rgonomic, **P**sychosocial, **S**afety

| Category | Definition | Examples (give 3-4) | Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | Environmental factors causing immediate physical harm | Noise, temperature extremes, radiation, slips/trips/falls, electricity, vibration, confined spaces, working at heights, moving machinery | Hearing loss, heatstroke/hypothermia, burns, fractures, electrocution, HAVS |
| **Chemical** | Substances harming via inhalation, ingestion, skin contact, absorption | Solvents (benzene, toluene), acids/bases (HCl, NaOH), pesticides, cleaning products (bleach, ammonia), flammable liquids, toxic dusts/fumes (asbestos, welding fume), heavy metals (lead, mercury) | Poisoning, burns, cancer, dermatitis, asphyxiation. **SDS = key resource** |
| **Biological** | Living organisms / byproducts causing disease | Bacteria (Salmonella, E. coli), viruses (Influenza, HBV, HIV, COVID-19), fungi/parasites (moulds, malaria), allergens (pollen, dander), bloodborne pathogens | Infection, allergy, chronic disease. Common in **healthcare, labs, agriculture, waste management** |
| **Ergonomic** | Poor workstation design, repetitive motion, awkward posture | Typing/assembly lines, bending & twisting, heavy lifting, prolonged sitting/standing, vibration from power tools, wrong chair/monitor height | **Musculoskeletal Disorders (MSDs)** — back injury, carpal tunnel |
| **Psychosocial** | Work-environment aspects harming mental health | Excessive workload, lack of job control, poor communication, bullying/harassment, poor work-life balance, job insecurity, shift work, long hours | Stress, anxiety, depression, **burnout** |
| **Safety** | Unsafe conditions/practices causing immediate injury | No machine guarding, exposed wires, fire hazards, no fall protection, confined-space entry without permit, forklift traffic, poor housekeeping | Cuts, crushing, falls, fire, fatalities. = **unsafe acts + unsafe conditions** |

💡 **Likely exam trap question:** "Which hazard category is most underestimated?" → Answer **Psychosocial**, because effects are invisible, gradual, hard to link causally to work, and stigma stops reporting — yet they drive absenteeism, turnover and error rates.

## 3. Safety Culture

**Definition:** The shared **values, beliefs and attitudes** in an organisation that influence safety-related behaviours — *"how we do things around here"* when it comes to safety.

### 7 Key elements — mnemonic **"LEC-CAMA"**
1. **L**eadership commitment — visible, active management support
2. **E**mployee participation — workers empowered to be involved
3. **C**ommunication — open, transparent sharing of safety information
4. **C**ontinuous learning — improve and learn from incidents
5. **A**ccountability — clear roles & responsibilities at all levels
6. **M**utual trust — everyone believes they share the same safety goals
7. **A**daptability — change practices as conditions/information change

### Why it matters
Foundation for all safety activity → shapes how risks are perceived & managed → greater engagement → collective responsibility → safer, healthier workplace. **Strong culture is proactive, not reactive.**

## 4. Accident Causation Theories (⭐ Q2e = 8 marks — learn all four)

### 4.1 Domino Theory (Heinrich)
Accidents = a **sequence of falling dominoes**. Five dominoes:
1. **Ancestry & social environment**
2. **Fault of person**
3. **Unsafe act or mechanical/physical hazard**
4. **Accident**
5. **Injury**

👉 **Key takeaway: remove ANY one domino — especially the 3rd — and the injury is prevented.**
*Example:* Worker removes a machine guard (unsafe act) → hand caught → laceration. Reinstating the guard removes domino 3.

### 4.2 Human Factors Theory
Accidents caused by **human error**, which arises from:
- **Overload** — physical/mental limits exceeded (fatigue, time pressure)
- **Incompatible workstation design** — poor ergonomics
- **Stress** — physical or psychological strain
👉 Design systems/tasks around human limitations.
*Example:* Nurse lifting patients on a 12-hour night shift (overload + poor design) → back injury.

### 4.3 Systems Theory
Accidents result from **interactions inside a complex system** — people, equipment, environment, management are interconnected. Accidents = **system failures**, not just individual mistakes. Analyse the whole system for root causes.
*Example:* Bhopal / Piper Alpha — design, maintenance, staffing, permits and management all failed together.

### 4.4 Energy Release Theory
Accidents = **uncontrolled release of energy** (kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, electrical). Injury occurs when the rate/amount of energy exceeds the body's tolerance.
👉 Prevention = control the energy source + place barriers between energy and people.
*Example:* Lockout/tagout before machine maintenance; guardrails to stop falls (potential energy).

## 5. Hierarchy of Controls (⭐ appears in EVERY exam — Q4b)

Most → least effective. Mnemonic: **"Every Smart Engineer Applies PPE last"**

| Rank | Control | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Elimination** | Physically remove the hazard | Remove the tripping obstacle entirely |
| 2 | **Substitution** | Replace with a safer alternative | Water-based paint instead of solvent-based |
| 3 | **Engineering controls** | Isolate people from the hazard | Machine guards, local exhaust ventilation, enclosure |
| 4 | **Administrative controls** | Change the way people work | Job rotation, permits, training, work/rest schedules, signage |
| 5 | **PPE** | Protect the individual | Gloves, respirators, goggles, hearing protection |

Always state: *"Prioritise higher-level controls wherever reasonably practicable; PPE is the last line of defence."*

## 6. Evolution of Safety Management (4 ages)
1. **Technological Age** — make the technology safe (Industrial Revolution)
2. **Human Factors Age** — recognise human error (post-Three Mile Island)
3. **Organisational Age** — management systems and culture
4. **Holistic / Systems Age** — safety integrated into everything

## 7. Technology: double-edged
- **Opportunities:** automate dangerous tasks, better monitoring, data-driven risk prediction
- **New hazards:** cybersecurity risk in connected systems, new ergonomic issues, mental strain from constant connectivity

## 8. Safety Performance Indicators (SPIs)

| | **Lagging** | **Leading** |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Past incidents & outcomes | Predicts future performance; preventive activity |
| Examples | Number of injuries, lost-time incident rate, workers' comp claims | Training sessions run, safety audits completed, employee participation rates |
| Nature | **Reactive**, easy to measure | **Proactive**, harder to measure |

Memory: **La**gging = **La**ter (already happened). **Lea**ding = **Lea**ds ahead.

---

## 9. Incident Investigation — 6 steps (⭐ memorise the order)

1. **Secure the scene** — preserve evidence, prevent further incidents
2. **Gather information** — evidence, witness interviews, documents
3. **Analyse data** — identify direct, contributing and **root** causes
4. **Develop corrective actions** — prevent recurrence
5. **Report findings** — document process & results
6. **Monitor implementation** — verify corrective actions actually happen

Mnemonic: **S-G-A-D-R-M** → *"Secure Good Answers, Deliver Real Momentum"*

---

## 10. Leadership, Participation, Communication, Improvement

- **Leadership sets the tone:** visible commitment · clear expectations · resources · accountability · recognise/reward safe behaviour
- **Employee participation channels:** safety committees · hazard reporting systems · training & meetings · **Job Safety Analysis (JSA)** · incentive programmes
- **Communication must be:** clear & concise · timely & relevant · accessible to all · **two-way**
- **Continuous improvement:** use the **PDCA cycle** (Plan-Do-Check-Act)

---

## 11. Importance of Safety & Risk Management (5 headings — ⭐ Q1b variant)

1. **Preventing injuries & illnesses** — legal *duty of care*; proactive beats reactive; objective is **zero harm**
2. **Improving legal/regulatory compliance** — meet OSHA/EPA/local standards; avoid fines, lawsuits, reputation damage
3. **Reducing costs** — lower workers' comp premiums; avoid direct (medical, lost wages) and indirect (property damage, lost productivity) costs
4. **Enhancing employee engagement & productivity** — workers who feel safeguarded feel valued → loyalty, retention, quality
5. **Protecting company reputation** — prevents brand damage, builds stakeholder trust, supports long-term sustainability
