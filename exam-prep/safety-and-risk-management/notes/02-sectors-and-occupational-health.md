## PART A — Three sectors (learn as: Challenges → Best Practices → Case study)

### 1. Manufacturing
**Challenges**
- *Physical:* complex machinery (moving parts, pinch points, sharp edges), electrical hazards, slips/trips/falls
- *Chemical:* dusts, metals, gases, vapours, fumes, aerosols; handling toxic chemicals
- *Other:* no machine guarding · inadequate **lockout/tagout** · poor ventilation → bad air quality · improper chemical handling/storage · inadequate training

**Best practices**
- Comprehensive safety programme, regularly reviewed
- Machine guarding with barriers + **safety interlocks**; regular maintenance & inspection
- Strict **lockout/tagout** before any maintenance (prevents accidental start-up)
- Effective ventilation — general + **local exhaust ventilation (LEV)**
- Proper chemical storage, handling, labelling + appropriate PPE

**Case studies (quote 1-2 for marks):** nonwoven manufacturer — **$2 M ROI** from machine safety upgrades · golf club manufacturer — **$222,000** return from ergonomic automation · food processor — **$100,000** ROI by eliminating ladders · pharmaceutical firm — **zero injuries** after ergonomic workstation redesign · timber workshop — wood dust cut below limits via ventilation.

### 2. Healthcare
**Challenges**
- *Chemical:* hazardous medications and disinfectants
- *Psychosocial:* work-related **stress and burnout** (long hours, demanding work); bullying and harassment
- *Other:* medical errors & delays from system complexity · insufficient staffing · poor team communication · emerging threats — counterfeit drugs, cybersecurity breaches
- *(Also examinable from the case study: **ergonomic** — manual patient handling → back injuries)*

**Best practices (10)**
1. **Safe patient handling programmes** — training + assistive devices (zero-lift policy, lift teams)
2. Clear communication protocols & teamwork
3. Slip/trip/fall prevention through environmental safety & training
4. Workplace-violence prevention protocols
5. Strict infection prevention & control (hand hygiene)
6. Stress management + mental health support
7. Continuous learning & training on new procedures/technology
8. Culture of safety encouraging open communication and **error reporting**
9. Leverage technology — EHRs, clinical decision support
10. Engage patients and families in care plans

**Case studies:** Valley View Hospital (vaccine distribution risk management via communication) · tailored violence response plans + drills · Houston Methodist Willowbrook (obstetric protocols via **simulation training**) · hospitals using OHM® · Harvard Medical Institutions (anaesthesia & obstetrics patient safety).

### 3. Service, Retail & Hospitality ⭐ (Q2b — Sri Lankan context)
**Challenges**
- *Physical:* slips, trips, falls in customer & work areas; **manual handling** injuries from lifting/moving stock
- *Psychosocial:* stress/anxiety/depression from customer interaction & workload; difficult or **aggressive customers** → abuse/violence; long, unpredictable hours → fatigue
- *Workplace violence* — bullying and harassment from customers, colleagues, managers
- *Other:* food service hazards (cuts, burns, chemical exposure) · high employee **turnover** weakens safety programmes · limited resources for safety in small businesses

**Best practices**
- Regular environmental checks — lighting, clear walkways, cable management
- Ergonomic assessments + manual handling training
- Stress management programmes and employee support services
- Clear policies for handling difficult customers incl. **de-escalation**
- Adequate staffing levels; flexible working arrangements

**Case studies:** Grand Hotel London (training + hazard reporting) · Amazon warehouses (**colour-coded floor markings**) · Mid & West Wales Fire & Rescue (**50% injury reduction**) · Countdown NZ (empowering teams) · Marriott Residence Inn (employee engagement).

📌 **Sri Lankan flavour to add in the exam:** tourism hotels in Kandy/Galle, supermarket chains, small family restaurants — informal employment, seasonal staff, minimal OSH budgets and low awareness (links to Lecture 4's SME/informal-sector challenges).

## PART B — Occupational Health

### Purpose (why it matters)
- Promote the highest **physical, mental and social well-being** of workers in all occupations
- **Prevent** health departures caused by working conditions; protect from risks
- **Adapt the work to the worker** (not the worker to the work) — inclusivity
- Enhance overall well-being; minimise consequences of occupational hazards
- Business-driven & holistic — safety is integral to organisational success and sustainability

### ⭐ 5 Principles of Occupational Health — **A-R-E-C-C**
| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A**nticipation | Proactively identify hazards during workplace **design** and process planning |
| **R**ecognition | Identify & understand existing and potential hazards |
| **E**valuation | Assess the level of exposure risk through monitoring & analysis |
| **C**ontrol | Implement measures to minimise/eliminate worker exposure |
| **C**onfirmation | Ongoing check that the controls actually work |

Memory: *"**A**lways **R**ecognise **E**very **C**ontrol's **C**onfirmation."*

### Recognition of workplace hazards — how
- Identify hazardous agents **and** who is exposed
- Know the agents, health risks and **exposure routes** — inhalation, ingestion, skin
- Steps: **workplace characterisation** (processes, materials, products, **SDS review**) → exposure patterns → hazard evaluation
- Do it during both **planning and operational** phases

*Restaurant-kitchen worked example (exam-ready):* **Anticipate** — kitchen layout puts fryer next to a walkway. **Recognise** — hot oil, wet floors, sharp knives, LPG, cleaning chemicals. **Evaluate** — how often staff pass the fryer, floor slip resistance, chemical SDS. **Control** — reposition fryer (engineering), anti-slip mats, non-slip shoes (PPE), decant chemicals safely (admin). **Confirm** — monthly inspections + incident trend review.

## PART C — Industrial Hygiene

**Definition:** the science of **anticipating, recognising, evaluating and controlling** environmental factors/stresses in the workplace that cause sickness. (Also called occupational hygiene / workplace health.)
- Primary focus: **preventing occupational diseases**
- Proactive & preventative
- Concern extends to the **community and the environment**

**Scope — 5 hazard types:** Chemical (dusts, metals, gases, vapours, fumes, aerosols) · Physical (noise, vibration, radiation, temperature, lighting, ergonomics) · Biological (bacteria, viruses, fungi) · Ergonomic (workstation design, repetitive motion) · increasingly **Psychosocial** (stress, workload, poor communication).

### Relationship: Occupational Health vs Industrial Hygiene (⭐ likely short question)
> Occupational **health** is the *broad discipline* covering total worker well-being.
> Industrial **hygiene** is a *specialised scientific branch inside it*, supplying the technical methods and tools to identify, evaluate and control environmental hazards, thereby preventing occupational disease.

Analogy: Occupational health = **medicine**; industrial hygiene = **the diagnostic lab** inside it.
Industrial hygienists assess and mitigate exposures with specialised expertise; strong collaboration between the two professions is essential for **total worker well-being**.
