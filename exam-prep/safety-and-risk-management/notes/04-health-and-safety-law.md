## 1. Why OSH matters — Moral, Legal, Business

### Moral imperative
- Employees have an undeniable **human right** to a safe and healthy working environment
- Employers bear an ethical duty to safeguard worker welfare
- 📌 **June 2022: the ILO enshrined "a safe and healthy working environment" as its FIFTH Fundamental Principle and Right at Work** — a headline fact worth quoting

### Legal imperative
- OSH laws impose statutory obligations — e.g. **UK Health and Safety at Work Act 1974**, **US OSH Act of 1970**
- Laws mandate: risk assessments, control measures, training, protective equipment
- **"Duty of care"** now includes **foreseeability** — you must proactively mitigate risks you *could* have foreseen

### Consequences of non-compliance
Criminal prosecution · hefty fines · **stop-work / prohibition notices** · imprisonment of senior executives. Regulators have authority to impose and enforce these.

### Business case (economic benefits)
- 📌 **Return of ~€2.2 for every €1 invested in OSH**
- **Direct costs:** medical treatment, workers' compensation, legal fees
- **Indirect costs: typically 2–4× higher than direct costs, sometimes up to 20×** — lost productivity, downtime, damaged reputation, recruiting/training replacements, higher insurance premiums, lower morale
- Enhanced productivity & morale; less sickness absence
- Improved reputation & competitive edge — attracts talent (Millennials, Gen Z) and socially conscious customers

## 2. International OSH Frameworks ⭐ (Q3d — "two key international organisations and their roles")

### 2.1 ILO — International Labour Organization
- A **specialised UN agency** setting global labour standards, including OSH
- Works through legally binding **Conventions** (binding upon ratification) and non-binding **Recommendations**
- **Influence:** ratified Conventions must be implemented through national legislation; even unratified standards guide collective agreements and case law; ILO provides practical implementation assistance to member states

**Key ILO OSH Conventions (memorise at least C155, C187, C081):**
| Category | No. | Name | Year | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fundamental | **C155** | Occupational Safety and Health Convention | **1981** | Coherent **national OSH policy**; prevention of accidents & injuries at government + enterprise level |
| Fundamental | **C187** | Promotional Framework for OSH Convention | **2006** | Promoting a national **culture of prevention** and OSH systems |
| Governance | **C081** | Labour Inspection Convention | **1947** | Role of labour inspection in enforcing labour laws |
| Governance | **C129** | Labour Inspection (Agriculture) Convention | **1969** | Labour inspection in agriculture |
| Governance | **C144** | Tripartite Consultation Convention | **1976** | **Tripartism** — collaboration between government, employers, workers in OSH policy |
| Technical | **C161** | Occupational Health Services Convention | **1985** | Accessible health services, medical examinations, first aid, health education |

### 2.2 ISO — International Organization for Standardization
- Develops **voluntary international standards**, including **ISO 45001** for OSH management systems

### 2.3 Regional & national implementation
International principles become enforceable law regionally (**EU Directives**) and nationally (**US OSH Act**). Variations reflect local context and legal tradition; some countries go more stringent.

*(Other bodies you can name for extra credit: **OSHA** — US enforcement agency; **HSE** — UK regulator; **NIOSH** — US research institute; **WHO** — health standards.)*

## 3. ISO 45001 (2018) ⭐

- International standard for **Occupational Health & Safety Management Systems (OHSMS)**
- Published **2018**, replacing **OHSAS 18001**
- Applicable to **any organisation**, any size or sector
- Uses the ISO **High-Level Structure (HLS)** → integrates easily with **ISO 9001** (quality) and **ISO 14001** (environment)

**Built on the PDCA cycle:**
| Stage | In ISO 45001 |
|---|---|
| **Plan** | Identify hazards, assess OSH risks, determine necessary controls |
| **Do** | Implement the controls and the OSH management system |
| **Check** | Monitor performance, investigate incidents, conduct audits and management reviews |
| **Act** | Take actions for continual improvement of OSH performance |

**Key components:** leadership commitment · worker involvement · hazard identification · risk evaluation · legal compliance · emergency preparedness · incident investigation.

⚠️ **Critical exam point — Certification ≠ legal compliance:**
> ISO 45001 **has no legal status** and is **not a substitute for national laws**. Certification demonstrates a legally compliant system and a commitment to improvement, but organisations must still comply fully with national law and collective agreements. ISO 45001 is an **"add-on"** that elevates OSH standards.

## 4. OSH in Sri Lanka ⭐

### 4.1 The Factories Ordinance No. 45 of 1942 (Q3e — 4 marks)
- **Primary legislation governing OSH in Sri Lanka**, enacted **1942**
- **Notably predates many modern global OSH frameworks** → signifies an early national commitment to worker protection
- Initially covered factories only; amendments extended coverage to **construction works, docks, wharves, quays, warehouses and ships**
- **Significant gaps:** no specific law on **chemical safety** or **ergonomics**

**Health provisions (Part II) — with section numbers (easy marks):**
| Provision | Section |
|---|---|
| Cleanliness | s.6 |
| Overcrowding | s.7 |
| Temperature | s.11 |
| Ventilation | s.12 |
| Lighting | s.13 |
| Drainage of floors | s.14 |
| Sanitary conveniences | s.15 |
| Medical supervision | s.16 |

**Welfare provisions (Part IV):**
| Provision | Section |
|---|---|
| Drinking water | s.46 |
| Washing facilities | s.47 |
| Accommodation for clothing | s.48 |
| Facilities for resting (female workers) | s.49 |
| First aid | s.50 |

*Memory hook:* Health = **6, 7, 11–16**; Welfare = **46–50**.

### 4.2 Other key Sri Lankan legislation
- **Workmen's Compensation Ordinance No. 19 of 1934**
- **Shop and Office Employees (Regulation of Employment and Remuneration) Act No. 19 of 1954**
- **Employment of Women, Young Persons & Children Act No. 47 of 1956**
- **National Environmental Act No. 47 of 1980**
- **National Policy on Elimination of Child Labour in Sri Lanka**
- Related: Electricity Act, Radiation Protection Act, Mines Act, Pesticide Act

### 4.3 Regulatory bodies
| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Ministry of Labour and Foreign Employment** | Overall responsibility for labour standards, enforcement, industrial peace, social protection; fostering a national safety culture |
| **Department of Labour (DoL)** | **Central agency for OSH administration and enforcement** |
| **NIOSH** — National Institute of Occupational Safety and Health | Established under **Act No. 38 of 2009**, began functioning **2011**. Provides world-class OSH services via **education, training, research and advisory services** |
| **Ministry of Health** | Workplace health, especially occupational diseases |
| **Central Environmental Authority (CEA)** | Ensures workplaces do not harm the environment |

### 4.4 Penalties
- **Employers:** hefty fines · operational suspension or permanent closure · reputational damage. Regulators may issue **improvement notices**, initiate legal proceedings, or order closure for serious/persistent violations
- **Employees:** warnings, suspension, termination
- **Legal basis:** workers have the right to know about hazards and to **refuse unsafe work without retaliation**

### 4.5 ⭐ Five challenges in Sri Lankan OSH implementation
1. **Outdated and ineffective regulations** — Factories Ordinance is old; many regulations still pending; gaps in chemical safety and ergonomics; legislative inertia vs modern hazards
2. **Deficiencies in SMEs and the informal sector** — the informal economy is **almost two-thirds of total employment**, with poor conditions and low OSH standards; informal construction faces falls from height, ladder/scaffolding issues due to financial constraints, lack of knowledge/skills, poor attitudes and weak regulation
3. **Lack of awareness, training and qualified personnel** — poor awareness among government, professionals, management, healthcare and employees; workplaces fail to implement OSHMS or do risk assessments; **shortage of qualified safety officers** (inadequate training facilities, low salaries, limited budgets, lack of recognition)
4. **Underreporting of accidents** — masks the true scale and hinders policy
5. **Fragmented enforcement** — multiple bodies (DoL, NIOSH) → duplication, inconsistency, wasted resources

### 4.6 Recent reforms & initiatives
- **National Guidelines for Bipartite OSH Committees (April 2023)** — nationwide rollout by the Ministry of Labour; committees of management + union/worker representatives; proactive safety culture, physical/mental health, better communication; **emphasises female representation**
- **"Safety for All" Programme** — targets small and medium factories; medical camps, OSH awareness programmes for factory workers and vocational-training students
- **International collaboration** — ILO's **"Safety + Health for All" Programme** builds OSH knowledge, capacity and national frameworks; involves workers' and employers' organisations; proposes leveraging occupational medicine expertise and strengthening labour inspectorate capability in hygiene and ergonomics

## 5. Landmark case studies (learn one line each — great for essay padding)

| Disaster | Year | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bhopal Gas Tragedy** (India) | 1984 | Methyl isocyanate leak from a Union Carbide pesticide plant killed thousands | Chemical process safety, failed safety systems, cost-cutting on maintenance; **Systems Theory** case |

## 6. Emerging trends in OSH

**Four drivers of change:** Digitalisation & AI · Mental health & psychosocial well-being · Climate change impacts · **ESG** integration.

### 6.1 Digitalisation & AI — Opportunities
- **Predictive safety analytics** — AI analyses historical + real-time data to anticipate risks; finds patterns humans miss (operator fatigue, equipment failure forecasts); shifts OSH **reactive → proactive**
- **Smart monitoring & sensors** — wearables detect fatigue and posture; environmental sensors track air quality; immediate alerts
- **Robotics & automation** — robots take the **"3D jobs"** (dirty, dangerous, demeaning); 24/7 monitoring; rapid hazard detection. 📌 **Every 10% increase in robot use → 0.066% decrease in workplace deaths and 1.96% reduction in injuries**
- **VR/AR** — immersive safety training
- **Compliance automation** — automated regulatory gap identification and audit reports

### 6.2 Digitalisation & AI — New Risks
- **Human–robot interaction** — unpredictable robot behaviour, system failures, cyber threats; trusting AI with human lives
- **Ergonomic risks** — from human-robot interaction and poorly fitting wearables/exoskeletons
- **Over-reliance on AI** — reduced human oversight
- **Algorithmic management & constant connectivity** — stress, burnout, mental health issues
- **Digital supply chain risks** — harm to workers in mineral extraction, AI infrastructure power, and **e-waste** handling

### 6.3 ESG integration in OSH
| Dimension | In OSH |
|---|---|
| **Environmental** | Reduce environmental hazards affecting worker safety; non-toxic eco-friendly materials, energy-efficient machinery; balance efficiency, safety and environmental impact |
| **Social** | Employee well-being, diversity and inclusion; safety culture valuing each individual |
| **Governance** | Transparent policies, accountability in safety management, ethical labour practices, OSH embedded in core decision-making |

**Benefits:** enhanced compliance and reduced risk · improved morale and public perception · stakeholder confidence · long-term growth · OSH performance becomes a key metric for **investors, consumers and talent**.
