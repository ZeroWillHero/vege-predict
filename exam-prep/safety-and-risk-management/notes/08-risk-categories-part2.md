# Lecture 8 — Risk Categories (Part 2): Terrorism, IT, Governance, People, Strategic, Environment, Food Hygiene

⏱ Study time: 45 min | 🔥 Exam weight: **MEDIUM** (supports Q4; also broad essay material)

> 🔁 **Every category follows the same 6-heading skeleton — memorise the skeleton, then just fill category-specific content:**
> **Definition → Characteristics → Impacts → Causes/Sources → Detection/Monitoring → Mitigation**

---

## 1. Terrorists and Extremists Risk

**Definition:** potential for harm, disruption or loss to an organisation, its assets, personnel or reputation due to **acts of terrorism or extremist violence**.

**Characteristics:** intentional & malicious (**ideological, political or religious motives**) · unpredictable, global reach · **high impact, low frequency** · **asymmetric threat** (unconventional methods against powerful targets) · aims at **disruption and fear** · legal & reputational dimensions (organisations implicated if products/services are misused or anti-terrorism-financing rules breached).

**Impacts:** loss of life/injury · physical damage to assets · business interruption & supply-chain disruption · financial loss · reputational damage · increased security costs · **psychological impact** (trauma and fear).

**Causes/Sources:** geopolitical instability · **radicalisation** · vulnerability of **"soft targets"** · misuse of products/services · **cyber terrorism** against critical infrastructure.

**Detection:** threat intelligence · geopolitical monitoring · behavioural analysis (insider threats) · financial transaction monitoring (**AML/CTF** programmes) · **OSINT** (open source intelligence — social media, public discussion).

**⭐ Mitigation:** robust physical security (access controls, surveillance, guards, blast-resistant measures) · emergency response & evacuation plans with training · business continuity & disaster recovery planning · enhanced cybersecurity · **AML & CTF compliance** · threat assessment & vulnerability analysis · employee training · collaboration with law enforcement/intelligence agencies · reputation management.

## 2. IT Risk

**Definition:** potential for business operations, assets or objectives to be negatively impacted by the **failure or compromise of IT systems, infrastructure, data or processes**.

**Characteristics:** **ubiquitous** (affects everything) · dynamic and evolving · interconnected · **high impact** · human element (error, negligence, malice) · heavy **regulatory scrutiny** (data privacy).

**Impacts:** data loss/corruption · system downtime/outages · financial losses (remediation, fines, ransomware, theft, insurance premiums) · reputational damage · **compliance violations (GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA)** · loss of competitive advantage (IP theft) · loss of productivity · **safety/physical harm in OT environments** (industrial control systems).

**Causes:** cybersecurity threats (malware, ransomware, spyware, phishing, DoS, insider threats, **SQL injection**, **XSS**) · system/hardware failure · software bugs/vulnerabilities · human error · natural disaster · lack of maintenance · **missing data backups** · insufficient access controls.

**Detection:** network monitoring tools · **SIEM** · **IDS/IPS** · vulnerability scanners & penetration testing · **EDR** (endpoint detection and response) · regular internal & external audits · system performance monitoring · **DLP** (data loss prevention) · incident response team & procedures.

**⭐ Mitigation:** comprehensive cybersecurity framework (firewalls, anti-malware, encryption, **MFA**, network segmentation) · **regular backups and DR planning** · patch & vulnerability management · strong access controls & identity management (**least privilege**, RBAC, access reviews) · security awareness training · tested **incident response plan** · redundancy & high availability · **vendor/third-party risk management** · compliance with IT regulations.

## 3. Governance Risk

**Definition:** potential for negative consequences from **deficiencies or failures in the organisation's framework for directing and controlling its operations**.

**Characteristics:** **systemic** (whole-organisation impact) · **top-down influence** · cultural element · compliance-driven · significant reputational impact.

**Impacts:** legal & regulatory penalties (anti-corruption, financial reporting) · reputational damage & **investor exodus** · loss of public confidence · financial losses · strategic failure · operational inefficiencies.

**Causes:** unclear roles & responsibilities · **ineffective board oversight** (passive board, no independent directors, insufficient challenge to management) · weak internal controls · **poor ethical culture / "tone at the top"** tolerating misconduct · non-compliance with laws · lack of transparency · **conflicts of interest**.

**Detection:** internal & external audits · compliance monitoring programmes · **board effectiveness reviews** · risk assessments · ethics & compliance hotlines · stakeholder feedback · legal reviews.

**⭐ Mitigation:** robust governance framework (clear structure, defined roles, lines of authority) · **independent and diverse board** with relevant expertise · strong internal control environment (financial, operational, IT) · ethical code of conduct + training · comprehensive compliance programme.

## 4. People Risk

**Definition:** potential for adverse impacts due to **human factors** — inadequate skills, poor performance, misconduct, health & safety issues, loss of key personnel, insufficient talent management.

**Characteristics:** human-centric · diverse sources and interconnected · impacts productivity & culture · reputational and legal exposure.

**Impacts:** reduced productivity (skill gaps, low morale, absenteeism) · **loss of key talent** · poor performance/quality · misconduct (harassment, discrimination, theft, ethical breaches) · increased costs (recruitment, training, legal settlements, benefits) · **workplace accidents/injuries** · reputational damage.

**Causes:** skill gaps / inadequate training · poor recruitment & selection · **high employee turnover** (weak retention, uncompetitive pay, poor environment) · lack of engagement/motivation · ineffective performance management · **inadequate health & safety measures** · workplace conflict/bullying/harassment · **stress & burnout** (excessive workload, poor work-life balance).

**Detection:** employee surveys (engagement, satisfaction) · performance reviews · **exit interviews** · absenteeism & turnover rates · workplace accident & incident reports · **HR analytics** · grievance systems/hotlines.

**⭐ Mitigation:** robust recruitment & onboarding · comprehensive training & development · effective performance management (feedback, clear goals, accountability, recognition) · competitive compensation & benefits · employee engagement initiatives · **strong health & safety programmes** (risk assessments, training, PPE, wellness) · clear policies & procedures · **succession planning** · conflict resolution mechanisms · leadership development · **employee well-being programmes** (mental health support, stress management).

📌 *Link to OSH:* People risk is where organisational risk management and occupational health & safety directly overlap — this is a great connection to make in an essay.

## 5. Strategic and Marketing Risk

**Definition:** potential for negative consequences to **long-term objectives, competitive position, market share or brand value**, arising from flawed strategic choices, ineffective execution, or unsuccessful marketing initiatives.

**Characteristics:** **long-term orientation** · high-level focus (direction, vision, positioning) · **external & internal drivers** (market dynamics, competition, regulation vs internal capability, resources, agility) · **difficult to quantify directly** · interconnected (strategic failure → financial, operational, reputational damage) · tied to **innovation and adaptability**.

**Impacts:** loss of market share · decline in revenue/profitability · reputational damage · failure to achieve growth targets · **competitive disadvantage** · wasted investment · loss of innovation leadership.

**Causes:** flawed market research/analysis · poor strategic planning (no clear vision, unrealistic goals, insufficient resources, inadequate scenario planning) · ineffective strategy execution / misalignment across departments · competitive actions (new entrants, aggressive pricing, superior launches) · **technological disruption** · regulatory changes · **changes in consumer behaviour/preferences** · brand mismanagement.

**Detection:** market share analysis · competitor analysis · customer satisfaction & loyalty metrics (surveys, **NPS**, retention) · sales performance & revenue growth vs targets · brand health metrics (awareness, perception, sentiment analysis) · strategic planning reviews · technological trend analysis.

**⭐ Mitigation:** robust strategic planning (market research, **scenario planning**, clear vision/mission/objectives) · **dynamic strategy review** · strong brand management (consistent messaging, proactive PR, customer service, ethics) · **customer-centric approach** with continuous feedback loops · investment in **R&D and innovation** · competitive intelligence · **market diversification** · **agile organisational structure**.

## 6. Environment Risk

**Definition:** potential for negative impacts on operations, reputation, financial performance or legal standing due to **direct or indirect interactions with the natural environment**.

**Characteristics:** interconnected & systemic (ecosystems and supply chains) · **long-term horizon** (climate change over decades but requires action now) · **regulatory & legal drivers** (environmental laws, treaties) · high **reputational sensitivity** · ⭐ **physical vs transition aspects** — physical impacts of climate change (extreme weather) vs risks from transitioning to a low-carbon economy (policy, market shifts) · resource-dependent.

**Impacts:** regulatory fines & penalties · legal action & lawsuits · reputational damage · operational disruptions · increased operating costs · **insurance premium increases or unavailability** · **investor pressure/divestment**.

**Causes:** pollution & emissions (air, water, soil) · resource depletion · climate change impacts · **transition risks** (policy, technology, market, reputational shifts) · non-compliance with laws and permits · waste management issues · **biodiversity loss & ecosystem degradation** · supply chain environmental risks · land use change (deforestation, habitat destruction).

**Detection:** **Environmental Impact Assessments (EIAs)** before new projects · environmental audits · **Environmental Management Systems (EMS) — ISO 14001** · emissions and discharge monitoring.

**⭐ Mitigation:** implement an **EMS (ISO 14001)** · pollution prevention & control · resource efficiency & conservation (**circular economy** principles) · climate change adaptation & mitigation · robust environmental compliance programme · **sustainable supply chain management**.

## 7. Food Hygiene Risk

**Definition:** potential for adverse health effects or negative business impacts arising from **contamination, spoilage or improper handling of food products** — leading to foodborne illness, allergic reactions or other food safety hazards.

**Characteristics:** **direct health impact** (illness or death) · extreme **reputational sensitivity** · heavy **regulatory scrutiny** · **supply chain complexity — "farm to fork"**, so **traceability is crucial** · high-volume impact (one event affects thousands) · **rapid spread** across geographies.

**Impacts:** foodborne illness outbreaks (sickness, hospitalisation, fatalities) · **product recalls** (costly, logistically hard) · regulatory fines (FDA, EFSA, local health departments) · legal action & lawsuits · severe reputational damage · business interruption · **loss of certifications/licences**.

**⭐ Causes — the 3 contaminant types (learn this trio):**
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Biological** | Bacteria (**Salmonella, E. coli, Listeria**), viruses (**Norovirus**), parasites, mould |
| **Chemical** | Pesticides, cleaning agents, unauthorised additives, heavy metals, allergen cross-contamination |
| **Physical** | Glass, metal shards, plastic pieces, hair, insects |

Plus: **improper temperature control** (inadequate cooking, cooling, hot holding, thawing) · **cross-contamination** (raw→cooked, dirty surfaces→food) · **poor personal hygiene** (unwashed hands, sick food handlers, improper attire) · inadequate cleaning & sanitation · contaminated raw materials from suppliers.

**Detection:** ⭐ **HACCP — Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points** · regular internal & external inspections/audits · **microbiological testing** of products, surfaces and water · temperature monitoring · supplier audits & verification · **traceability systems** · employee health monitoring.

**⭐ Mitigation:** implement a robust food safety management system — **HACCP, ISO 22000 or GFSI-recognised standards** · comprehensive and continuous **employee training** (food hygiene, personal hygiene, cross-contamination, safe handling) · **strict temperature control** for cooking, cooling, storage and transport · effective cleaning & sanitation programmes · **supplier management** (vetting + monitoring) · **allergen control** (dedicated lines, clear labelling, strict cleaning protocols).
