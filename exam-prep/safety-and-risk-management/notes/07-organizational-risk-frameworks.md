## 1. Organizational Risk

**Definition:** the potential for an event or action to occur that could **negatively impact an organisation's ability to achieve its objectives, fulfil its mission, or maintain its value**.

- Risks are inherent in **every facet** of operations — from strategic planning and daily tasks to external interactions with customers, suppliers and regulators
- Effective risk management shifts focus from **reacting to problems** → to **proactively identifying threats, assessing likelihood and impact, and preventing/mitigating them before significant harm**
- This forward-looking approach is crucial for **resilience**

**Two prominent frameworks:** **ISO 31000:2018** and **COSO ERM**.

---

## 2. ⭐⭐ ISO 31000:2018 — Risk Management Guidelines (Q4d — 6 marks)

**What it is:** an international standard from the **International Organization for Standardization**, providing **principles and generic guidelines** for managing risk in any organisation, regardless of type, size, industry or sector. It is **principles-based** — flexible, not prescriptive — so organisations tailor it to their needs.

### Characteristics
- **Universal applicability** — any organisation, all forms of risk
- **Principles-based** — guidelines and a framework rather than requirements
- **Focus on integration** — risk management embedded in governance, strategy, planning, management, reporting, policies, values and **culture**
- **Value creation and protection** — the core purpose
- **Iterative process**

### ⭐ The THREE key components (this is Q4d — 6 marks, 2 marks each)

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Principles** | The **foundation** for managing risk effectively and creating value — high-level guiding statements. Risk management should: **create and protect value**; be an **integral part of organisational processes**; be **part of decision-making**; explicitly **address uncertainty**; be **systematic, structured and timely**; be based on the **best available information**; be **tailored**; take **human and cultural factors** into account; be **transparent and inclusive**; be **dynamic, iterative and responsive to change**; and **facilitate continual improvement**. |
| **2. Framework** | The set of components providing the **foundations and organisational arrangements** for **designing, implementing, monitoring, reviewing and continually improving** risk management throughout the organisation. (i.e. leadership & commitment, integration, design, implementation, evaluation, improvement.) |
| **3. Process** | The **systematic application** of management policies, procedures and practices to the activities of: **communicating and consulting · establishing the context · identifying · analysing · evaluating · treating · monitoring and reviewing** risk. |

🧠 **Memory model:** *Principles = **WHY** (the value) · Framework = **WHO/HOW it's organised** (the structure) · Process = **WHAT you actually do** (the steps).*

**ISO 31000 process, in order (write this as a flow):**
```
Communication & Consultation  ──┐   (continuous, alongside everything)
```

## 3. ⭐⭐ COSO ERM (Q4c — "specify any four components" — 4 marks)

**What it is:** the **Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission — Enterprise Risk Management** framework. Introduced **2004**, updated **2017** as *"Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance."* More **structured and comprehensive**, explicitly linking risk management to **strategy and objectives**.

**Emphases:** focus on strategy and performance · organised around **five interrelated components** and supporting principles · value creation and preservation · governance and culture. **Widely adopted in North America**, common with publicly traded firms and those focused on financial controls (origin in financial reporting and auditing).

### ⭐ The 5 components of COSO ERM 2017 (name any 4 for the exam)
1. **Governance and Culture**
2. **Strategy and Objective-Setting**
3. **Performance**
4. **Review and Revision**
5. **Information, Communication and Reporting**

🧠 Mnemonic: **"Good Strategy Performs, Review It"** → **G-S-P-R-I**

*One-line explanations if marks require them:*
- **Governance & Culture** — the board's oversight, operating structures, desired culture, core values, attracting/developing capable people
- **Strategy & Objective-Setting** — analyse the business context, define risk appetite, evaluate alternative strategies, formulate business objectives
- **Performance** — identify, assess and prioritise risks, implement risk responses, develop a portfolio view
- **Review & Revision** — assess substantial change, review risk and performance, pursue improvement
- **Information, Communication & Reporting** — leverage information systems, communicate risk information, report on risk, culture and performance

## 4. ⭐ ISO 31000 vs COSO ERM — comparison table (memorise 4 rows)

| Aspect | **ISO 31000** | **COSO ERM** |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin/Scope** | International (ISO), broad guidelines | Primarily U.S. (COSO), integrates with strategy & performance |
| **Approach** | **Principles-based**, flexible, adaptable | **Structured model** with defined components and principles |
| **Primary focus** | Generic guidelines for managing risk | Integrating risk management with strategy and objectives |
| **Detail level** | More concise (guidelines) | More detailed and comprehensive |
| **Applicability** | Any organisation, any risk type | Any organisation; favoured for financial/internal control |
| **Geographic trend** | Global adoption | More prevalent in North America |
| **Core emphasis** | **Creating and protecting value** | **Achieving performance and profitability targets** |

## 5. The 11 Risk Categories (know the list; details for the big four below)

**Procurement · Security · Financial · Operations and Products · IT · Governance · People · Ethical · Strategic and Marketing · Environment · Food Hygiene**

Definitions in one line each:
- **Procurement risk** — acquiring goods, services or works
- **Security risk** — threats to physical assets, information and personnel
- **Financial risk** — dangers to financial health and stability
- **Operations & products risk** — issues in daily processes and product/service delivery
- **IT risk** — vulnerabilities in IT systems and data
- **Governance risk** — challenges in leadership, structure and compliance
- **People risk** — human capital and workforce management
- **Ethical risk** — dilemmas over moral principles and organisational values
- **Strategic & marketing risk** — threats to business strategy, market position and brand
- **Environment risk** — environmental factors and sustainability
- **Food hygiene risk** — food safety and public health

## 6. Security Risk

**Definition:** potential for **loss, damage or unauthorised access** to an organisation's assets (physical, information, human, intangible) due to **malicious acts, accidental breaches, or failures in security controls**.

**Characteristics:** broad scope (physical locations, digital systems, IP, personnel) · **dynamic** (threats constantly evolve) · often **intentional** (cybercriminals, terrorists, disgruntled employees) but can be accidental · **interconnected** (one breach ripples into finance and reputation) · targets the **CIA Triad — Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability**.

**Impacts:** financial losses (theft, ransomware payments, fines, remediation, downtime revenue loss) · reputational damage · operational disruption (outages, data corruption) · legal/regulatory penalties · loss of IP · harm to individuals (identity theft, privacy violation, physical injury).

**Causes/Sources:** cyber attacks (malware, phishing, DoS) · physical breaches (unauthorised facility access, equipment theft) · human error/negligence (weak passwords, lost devices, social engineering) · system vulnerabilities (bugs, unpatched systems) · insider threats · supply-chain vulnerabilities · natural disasters.

**Detection/Monitoring:** **SIEM** systems · **IDS/IPS** · vulnerability scanners & penetration testing · physical security audits · employee training & awareness programmes · access control monitoring · threat intelligence feeds.

**⭐ Mitigation:** robust access controls (**MFA**, role-based access, **least privilege**) · regular security updates & **patch management** · **data encryption** · firewalls & network segmentation · security awareness training + physical security measures · **incident response plan** · regular backups & disaster recovery.

## 7. Financial Risk

**Definition:** potential for **monetary loss** or negative impact on financial health, performance and stability — uncertainties around market fluctuations, creditworthiness, liquidity and operational financial management.

**Characteristics:** **quantifiable** (measurable in money) · **systemic** (broad market) vs **specific/idiosyncratic** (one organisation) · interconnected (poor liquidity worsens credit risk) · **forward-looking** · directly affects profitability and solvency.

**Impacts:** loss of revenue/profitability · **liquidity crisis** → potential bankruptcy · increased borrowing costs · decline in asset value · negative cash flow · **bankruptcy**.

**⭐ Causes/Sources (the classic list):**
- **Market risk** — interest rates, exchange rates, commodity prices, equity prices
- **Credit risk** — counterparties failing to meet obligations
- **Liquidity risk** — can't sell assets fast enough / insufficient cash flow
- **Operational financial risk** — transaction errors, fraud, weak controls, poor budgeting
- **Inflation risk** · **Interest rate risk** · **Regulatory/compliance risk** · **economic downturns** · **poor capital management**

**Detection:** financial statement analysis · **KPIs** (debt-to-equity, current ratio, quick ratio, gross/net profit margin, cash conversion cycle) · credit monitoring · market analysis · **stress testing & scenario analysis** · budget vs actual analysis · internal & external audits.

**⭐ Mitigation:** **diversification** · **hedging** (futures, options) · robust credit policies (credit checks, limits, timely collection) · maintain adequate liquidity (cash reserves, credit lines) · strong internal financial controls (**segregation of duties**, authorisation, reconciliations) · conservative capital structure · strategic financial planning · **insurance** (business interruption) · regular review of financial contracts.

## 8. Operations and Products Risk

**Definition:** potential for loss from **inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events**, in day-to-day activities, production and service delivery — including risks in the design, manufacturing, distribution and use of products/services.

**Characteristics:** primarily **internal focus** · **process-centric** · significant **human element** · **system dependence** · **direct impact on customer satisfaction**.

**Impacts:** service disruptions/outages · product defects & **recalls** (warranty claims, lawsuits) · customer dissatisfaction and churn · reputational damage · financial losses and regulatory fines · loss of market share · employee morale issues.

**Causes:** process failures (inefficient workflows, no standard procedures, poor documentation, control breakdowns) · human error · system failures · **supply chain disruptions** · equipment malfunctions · inadequate product design · quality control issues · external events (natural disasters, pandemics, infrastructure failure) · internal operational **fraud** · compliance breaches.

**Detection:** operational **KPIs** (production output, defect rates, service response times, complaint rates, downtime) · process audits · supplier performance reviews · quality control inspections · customer feedback channels · incident reporting systems · system monitoring tools · training effectiveness reviews.

**⭐ Mitigation:** process optimisation & standardisation · robust **Quality Management Systems** (ISO standards, **Six Sigma**, **Lean**) · employee training & competency development · **redundancy and Business Continuity Planning (BCP)** · preventative maintenance · strong supply chain management (diversify suppliers, strong contracts) · effective product testing & validation (**UAT**) · **automation** to reduce human error · clear communication channels · insurance (product liability, business interruption).

---

## 9. Fraud Risk

**Definition:** potential for financial or reputational loss due to **intentional deception, misrepresentation or dishonesty** by individuals for personal gain or to harm the organisation.

**Characteristics:** always an **intentional act** · **concealment** · motivated by financial gain (also power, prestige, grievance) · ⭐ arises from **opportunity + rationalisation** — the **FRAUD TRIANGLE** (*Pressure/Incentive + Opportunity + Rationalisation*) · internal or external · **evolving methods**, often leveraging technology.

**Impacts:** direct financial loss (theft of cash, assets, inventory, unauthorised payments) · reputational damage · legal & regulatory penalties · operational disruption · **erosion of employee trust and morale** · increased costs · loss of IP.

**Causes:** **weak internal controls** (no segregation of duties, poor authorisation and oversight, insufficient reconciliation) · lack of employee training/awareness · **collusion** and **management override** · information asymmetry · technological vulnerabilities · economic pressure · high turnover · complex transactions · external pressure/sophistication.

**Detection:** **internal audits** · **data analytics** (anomaly/pattern detection) · fraud detection software · **whistleblower hotlines** · background checks · **segregation of duties (SOD) monitoring** · regular reconciliation · physical & digital surveillance.

**⭐ Mitigation:** strong internal control environment (SOD, authorisation limits, reconciliations, independent verification) · **anti-fraud policy & code of conduct** · fraud awareness training · **whistleblower protection programme** · data analytics & AI-powered fraud detection · employee vetting & background checks · **rotation of duties** · **"tone at the top"** — ethical culture from senior management · robust incident response plan.
